# HR Analytics — Employee Retention Recommendations

## 1. Objective

The objective of this document is to translate employee attrition analysis into practical retention strategies that HR stakeholders can evaluate.

Recommendations should be prioritized using verified SQL results, Power BI findings, and additional organizational evidence.

## 2. Recommended Actions

### 1. Investigate High-Attrition Departments

**Analytical signal:** A department has a higher attrition rate than other departments.

**Recommended action:**
- Review workload distribution and team capacity.
- Conduct structured exit interviews.
- Examine employee engagement and manager feedback.
- Compare attrition trends over time.

**Success metric:** Department-level attrition rate and employee exits.

### 2. Review High-Attrition Job Roles

**Analytical signal:** Certain job roles show comparatively high attrition.

**Recommended action:**
- Review compensation competitiveness and career progression.
- Evaluate training opportunities and promotion pathways.
- Assess workload expectations and role clarity.
- Gather feedback from current and former employees.

**Success metric:** Attrition rate by job role and internal promotion rate.

### 3. Evaluate Overtime and Workload

**Analytical signal:** Employees working overtime have a higher attrition rate than employees who do not.

**Recommended action:**
- Review overtime frequency and workload allocation.
- Identify teams with persistent overtime requirements.
- Assess staffing levels and operational bottlenecks.
- Pilot workload-balancing measures where appropriate.

**Success metric:** Overtime frequency and attrition rate by overtime status.

### 4. Improve Employee Experience

**Analytical signal:** Lower job satisfaction or work-life balance ratings coincide with higher attrition.

**Recommended action:**
- Collect regular employee feedback.
- Review workload flexibility and manager support.
- Identify recurring concerns through surveys and exit interviews.
- Track whether targeted interventions improve employee experience.

**Success metric:** Satisfaction ratings, work-life balance ratings, and attrition rate.

### 5. Monitor Age and Tenure Patterns

**Analytical signal:** Attrition differs across age groups or years of service.

**Recommended action:**
- Examine onboarding and early-career support.
- Review development opportunities at different career stages.
- Assess access to training and internal mobility.
- Avoid assumptions about individual employees based on age.

**Success metric:** Attrition rate by tenure band and age group.

## 3. Prioritization Framework

Prioritize actions using three considerations:

1. **Attrition rate:** How high is the rate compared with other groups?
2. **Business impact:** How many employees are affected, and what operational impact could result?
3. **Actionability:** Can the organization investigate or address the potential issue?

High attrition rates should be evaluated alongside group size. A small group with an unusually high rate may need a different response from a large group with a moderately elevated rate.

## 4. Measuring Success

After an intervention, compare outcomes against a defined baseline and an appropriate follow-up period.

Recommended measures include:

- Overall attrition rate
- Employee exits by department and job role
- Attrition by overtime status
- Attrition by tenure band
- Employee satisfaction indicators
- Retention outcomes following targeted interventions

Where possible, use comparable time periods and account for changes in workforce composition before concluding that an intervention was effective.

## 5. Role of AI

AI-assisted analysis can help summarize findings, organize potential explanations, and draft recommendations.

AI-generated recommendations should be reviewed by HR stakeholders and validated against actual business conditions. They should not be presented as proven solutions without evidence.

## 6. Conclusion

A data-driven retention strategy combines reliable measurement, investigation of workforce patterns, targeted interventions, and continuous monitoring.

The objective is not merely to identify employees who leave, but to help the organization understand where further investigation and improvement may have the greatest value.
